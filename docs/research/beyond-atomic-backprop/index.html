Backpropagation
is not atomic.

A completed experimental study of decoupling activation-gradient transport from weight-gradient evaluation to reduce the cost of neural-network pretraining without breaking its gradient highway.

Skipping a layer should not mean dropping its gradient transport.

Transformer backpropagation performs two different calculations: the activation gradient that carries learning signals to earlier layers, and the weight gradient that updates a layer’s parameters. Prior selective-backpropagation approaches treat them as an indivisible block. Our experiments show that this can destabilize pretraining when layers rotate in and out of the backward pass.

Decouple transport from parameter updates

Decoupled Sparse Backpropagation preserves the activation-gradient path (dX) through every layer at every step. It selectively omits the more expensive parameter-gradient calculation (dW) on layers whose optimizer trajectory is sufficiently stable, using a proprietary method to handle skipped updates. The schedule uses non-adjacent, strided layer sets rather than contiguous frozen blocks.

forward → exact dX transport → selective dW → optimizer update

What the completed benchmark measured

On a 16-layer, 34.58M-parameter Deep TinyGPT model trained for 1,000 steps, the four-way strided schedule removed 18.8% of dW evaluations with a +0.10% final-loss gap against full backpropagation. A strided parity schedule removed 36.7% with a +0.34% gap. These are controlled benchmark results from the stated hardware, framework, model, and training run—not a general claim about all architectures or workloads.

A proprietary update method

The method for handling skipped parameter updates is proprietary. Its role is narrow: preserve stable training behavior while the activation-gradient path remains exact. The public result is the measured training outcome, not a disclosure of the update-prediction mechanism.

What remains open

The work establishes a mechanism and a measured small-model result. It does not yet establish equivalent quality or wall-clock gains for multi-billion-parameter training runs, other optimizers, other architectures, or every distributed stack. The 7B–70B communication figures in the working paper are network-traffic projections under stated assumptions, not completed large-scale experiments.
