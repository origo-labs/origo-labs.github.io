The tool call is not the intelligence.

Much of the System-1 excitement points at a real category: applications often need a finite decision, not an essay. A ticket must reach a queue. An alert must be scored. A request must be approved, escalated, or declined.

Jev makes that category visible with three primitives: Choice, Score, and Noul. They select from a defined set, place a state on an ordered scale, or evaluate a yes-or-no statement. The important idea is not that a proprietary model has made software decisions mysterious. It is that the useful output is often structured and small.

Separate interpretation from authority

Our prototype studies a local System-1 component built around GLiNER2.5. Its semantic layer turns a message, ticket, alert, or command into a typed interpretation: intent, relevant entities, route, and confidence. A separate policy layer then checks thresholds, approval requirements, supported operations, and idempotency.

message → intent + entities → policy → route or action

That boundary is the point. Correct classification does not grant permission to execute. Uncertain or unsupported inputs can abstain; high-risk inputs can escalate; only explicitly allowed, low-risk routes can proceed through an opted-in adapter.

Why local is worth testing

For frequent, narrowly defined decisions, local inference can remove a per-request API fee and network dependency while keeping the semantic step within the organization’s boundary. It does not make inference free, eliminate integration work, or establish a universal accuracy claim. Hardware, maintenance, model choice, and the quality of the schema still matter.

What remains to be proven

The research is not a claim that small models replace general-purpose models for writing, synthesis, research, or planning. It is a narrower question: for a specific operational workflow, can a compact local model and typed policy be accurate enough, abstain safely, and meet the required latency?

Answering it requires a customer-shaped fixture set and reproducible measurements of intent and entity quality, abstention, policy safety, cold and warm latency, and throughput. The prototype provides the contract and evaluation path; each deployment still needs to earn its result.
