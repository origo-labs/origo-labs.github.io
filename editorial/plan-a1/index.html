Executive summary

Biology has a language of silence.

Single-cell RNA sequencing measures up to roughly 33,000 genes per cell, while most genes are structurally zero. Dense Transformer pipelines commonly reduce that space to a short list of highly variable genes (HVGs), trading tractability for the loss of rare markers and long-tail regulatory programs.

That is the central problem behind Plan-A1. The architecture treats a zero as information rather than empty space: a dormant gene should not acquire a faint artificial signal merely because a normalization layer centered the rest of the cell. The result is both an argument about how single-cell models should be built and an experimental report of where that choice helps—and where it does not.

Plan-A1 is an open-sourced zero-preserving sparse Transformer designed to keep those zeros meaningful. Its representation path uses top-K coordinate gating, mean-preserving RMS normalization, Dual-Stream Feature Routing, and Global Hub Tokens.

What was built

  • Zero-preserving top-K gating: unexpressed genes remain exact mathematical zeros, while expressed genes retain sparse coordinate supports.
  • Mean-preserving RMSNorm: scales coordinates without subtracting a mean, so neighboring activity does not turn silence into activation.
  • Dual-Stream Feature Routing: separates cell identity from dynamic pathway and perturbation signals.
  • Global Hub Tokens: provide bounded cross-module communication without making every gene attend to every other gene.

Finding 1 — rare-lineage marker protection

On 10X PBMC-3k cell typing with frozen backbones and linear probing, Plan-A1 reached Macro-F1 of 0.5454 versus 0.4101 for the dense scGPT baseline. For rare lineages below 3% frequency, it reached F1 of 0.3077 versus 0.1111, a reported relative gain of 19.66 percentage points.

Finding 2 — hubs help a controlled causal task

On synthetic causal graphs with 20 perturbation conditions, the H=4 hub-token configuration reached OOD Delta MSE of 0.05800, slightly below the dense baseline at 0.05806. The article reports that hubs also reduced pretraining time from 52.5 seconds to 43.6 seconds in that setup.

Where sparsity loses

On the Norman 2019 real-world CRISPRa benchmark, the dense baseline performed better under the short, minimally pretrained setup: OOD Delta MSE of 0.00177 versus 0.00571 for Plan-A1 without hubs. Increasing the hub count to 16 worsened the result to 0.00712.

This is the inductive sparsity trade-off. Dense attention can memorize correlations quickly on a pre-filtered 1,500-gene panel. Sparse coordinate routing needs enough data and training time to learn useful biological topology. Plan-A1 is therefore aimed at full-transcriptome scale and rare-cell representation, not every short perturbation task.

What this establishes

The reported results support a specific claim: preserving structural zeros can improve rare-lineage representation while making a full-transcriptome design plausible. They do not establish universal superiority over dense models, clinical utility, or a solved causal-perturbation model.

Evidence note. Independent replication, broader datasets, and external validation remain open.
